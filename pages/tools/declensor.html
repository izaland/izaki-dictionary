§ Grammar Reference 文法参照 — Noun Declension 名詞曲用

1. Overview 概要

Izaki nouns inflect for eleven cases and two numbers (singular and plural). The declension system distinguishes two principal classes of nouns:

  • Native words (origin: native) — subject to the full system of stem-internal alternations described in §3.
  • Byakuzhi words 百語 (origin: byakuzhi) — Sino-Izaki loanwords with an immutable root; cases are formed analytically by suffix addition, with no sonorisation or vowel lowering (§5).

3. Stem Alternations in Native Words 固有語幹交替

In native Izaki nouns, the oblique cases GEN, ESS, ALL, and ABL are built not on the citation form but on a derived strong stem, produced by the following ordered rules. The remaining cases — NOM, ACC, DAT, INS, PRO, TER, BEN — use the weak stem (= citation form).

Rule R4 — Evanescent velar insertion

Citation forms ending in -ae or -oe insert an epenthetic k before the final vowel in the strong stem: tae → take-, koe → koke-. R4 takes priority over all subsequent rules.

Rule R2 — Degemination

A geminate consonant immediately preceding the final vowel (or at the end of a consonant-final stem) is reduced to its singleton: kk→k, tt→t, pp→p, ss→s, cch→ch, tts→ts, ssh→sh, ll→l, nn→n.

Citation formStrong stemRule fired
akko 'stone'ako-R2: kk→k
attei 'river'ate-R2 + R3

Rule R3 — Vowel lowering i → e

In bisyllabic native words whose final vowel (weak stem) is -i or -ī, the strong stem lowers that vowel to -e. When R3 is triggered after R2 has already fired (degemination chain), Rule R1 is blocked.

Rule R1 — Sonorisation

The final obstruent of the consonant body is voiced when preceded by a vowel, n, or l. Multi-character digraphs (ch, ts, sh) take precedence over single-character matches. R1 is blocked when the R2+R3 chain has already fired.

4.2 Plural Oblique Cases 複数斜格

All plural oblique cases (GEN through BEN, excluding NOM) are built directly on the singular nominative stem (citation form), not on the plural nominative. For vowel-final nouns the suffix is attached directly; for consonant-final nouns an epenthetic -e- is inserted first.

5. Byakugo (百語) Analytic Declension

Nouns of Byakuzhi (Sino-Izaki) origin — marked origin: byakuzhi in the lexicon — follow a simplified, agglutinative paradigm. The root never undergoes internal mutation. The plural is formed uniformly with the suffix -ta; oblique plurals use the -(k)i- base (with k-insertion after -i finals).
