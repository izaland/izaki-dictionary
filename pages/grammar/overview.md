# Grammar Overview

## Introduction

Izaki (伊崎語, *Izakigo*) is a language isolate spoken primarily in Izaland and parts of South-Eastern Uletha, with an estimated 132 million speakers. The language exhibits agglutinative morphology and features a strict SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) constituent order. Izaki is regulated by the Izaland National Academy for the Izaki Language and possesses two recognized major dialects: Warohan and Juhuku.

Unlike many languages in its geographic region, Izaki is non-tonal, though it demonstrates phonemic vowel length and a complex system of consonant gradation. The language employs a mixed writing system consisting of logographic characters (*byakuzhi* 百字) borrowed from Bai, and an indigenous syllabary (*askaoza* 明字).

## Phonology

### Vowel System

Izaki possesses a five-vowel system with phonemic length distinction. Each vowel may occur in either short or long form, yielding ten distinct vocalic phonemes:

| Position | Front | Central | Back |
|----------|-------|---------|------|
| Close | i, iː | y | u, uː |
| Mid | e, eː | | o, oː |
| Open | | a, aː | |

The central close vowel /y/ occurs primarily in the Wendmark-Dzenkuku dialect and northern varieties of Izaland. In standard Izaki, this phoneme is largely absent.

**Orthographic Conventions:**
- Short vowels: *a, e, i, o, u*
- Long vowels: *ā, ē, ī, ō, ū* (Sanotari-Idanos romanization) or *aa, ee, ii, oo, uu* (Tagenas romanization)

### Syllable Structure

Izaki phonotactics are highly constrained, requiring all syllables to conform to one of the following templates:

1. **V** - Any vowel (short or long): *a*, *ō*
2. **CV** - Consonant + vowel: *ka*, *mi*
3. **CVF** - Consonant + vowel + final consonant: *kan*, *mir*

**Permissible final consonants (F):** Only /n/, /l/, /s/, /r/, /h/, and geminate /kː/ may occupy the coda position. The geminate /kː/ appears exclusively in southwestern dialects, notably in the toponym *Kikkansu* (Itakiri Prefecture).

**Phonotactic Constraints:**
- No syllable may begin with a consonant cluster
- No word-final /h/ is permitted
- Foreign loanwords must be adapted to conform to native syllable structure

### Diphthongs

Izaki permits the semivowels /j/ (orthographic *y*) and /w/ to form rising diphthongs with the five primary vowels. These are represented orthographically as: *ya, ye, yo, yu, wa, we, wi, wo*. The sequence /wj/ + V is attested in limited contexts, as in the compound *wja*.

### Morphophonemic Processes

#### Consonant Gradation (Sonorization)

In nominal and verbal morphology, voiceless obstruents undergo sonorization in certain morphological environments, particularly preceding sonorant-initial suffixes:

| Voiceless | Voiced |
|-----------|--------|
| k | g |
| p | b |
| t | d |
| s | z |
| ts | dz |
| tʃ | dʒ |
| ʃ | ʒ |

**Example:** *lota* "sheet" → *lodas* (essive case)

This process is blocked when:
- The consonant is geminated
- The consonant follows /h/ or /s/

**Example:** *dahkei* "precipice" → *dahkein* (genitive, no gradation)

#### Nasal Assimilation

The nasal /n/ in coda position assimilates in place of articulation to a following consonant:

- Before velars /k, g/: [ŋ] - *kan ko* → [kaŋko]
- Before bilabials /p, b/: [m] - *kan ban* → [kamban]

This assimilation is purely phonetic and does not affect orthography.

#### Final Consonant + Consonant Interactions

**Final /s/ + Consonant:**

The sibilant /s/ exhibits complex assimilation patterns:

- /s/ + /p, b/ → [sp]
- /s/ + /t, d/ → [st]
- /s/ + /k, g/ → [sk]
- /s/ + /tʃ, dʒ, ts, z, dz/ → [tːʃ] or [tːs] (gemination)
- /s/ + /ʃ, ʒ/ → [ʃː]
- /s/ + /h/ → [ʃ]

**Final /r/ or /l/ + Liquid:**

When a lateral or rhotic final precedes the other liquid, full assimilation occurs:
- /l/ + /r/ → [lː] - *kul ren* → [kullen]
- /r/ + /l/ → [lː] - *tar li* → [talli]

**Final /h/ + Consonant:**

The glottal fricative triggers devoicing of following voiced obstruents:
- *mah do* → [mahto]
- *kuih ge* → [kuihke]

When /h/ precedes another /h/, both consonants merge to form [pː]:
- *mah hari* → [mappari]

#### Final Consonant + Vowel

When a final consonant immediately precedes a vowel-initial morpheme, the following processes apply:

1. **/n, l, s/ + V** → consonant gemination:
   - *kan an* → [kannan]
   - *mas yn* → [massyn]

2. **/h/ + V** → elision of /h/ with compensatory vowel lengthening:
   - *tuh is* → [tuːis]

3. **/r/ + V** → /r/ → [t]:
   - *ner oi* → [netoi]

## Writing System

Izaki employs a hybrid orthography combining logographic *byakuzhi* (百字) characters derived from Bai script with the indigenous syllabic *askaoza* (明字) alphabet. This digraphic system functions comparably to the Japanese kanji-kana relationship.

### Functional Distribution

- **Byakuzhi**: Employed for lexical morphemes (nouns, verb stems, adjective roots) to convey semantic content
- **Askaoza**: Used for grammatical morphemes (case markers, verbal inflections, particles) and phonetic disambiguation

### Askaoza Principles

1. **Syllabic representation**: Each character corresponds to one mora
2. **Default vowel**: Consonant characters inherently carry the vowel /a/; other vowels are indicated via diacritics
3. **Voicing distinction**: Voiced obstruents are marked with a superscript dot
4. **Vowel diacritics**:
   - /e/: right diacritic
   - /i/: vertical bar
   - /o/: small circle (top-right)
   - /u/: curved element (resembling an umbrella handle)

5. **Vowel length**: A rightward diacritic (長音符 *chōonfu*) marks long vowels

## Nominal Morphology

### Number

Izaki nouns distinguish singular and plural number through suffixation:

**Vowel-final stems:**
- Singular: base form
- Plural: vowel lengthening + *-n*
  - *miwa* "water" → *miwaan* "waters"
  - *suto* "man" → *sutoon* "men"

**Consonant-final stems:**
- Singular: base form  
- Plural: consonant gemination + echo vowel + *-n*
  - *pyomyen* "surface" → *pyomyenne* "surfaces"
  - *moigon* "wave" → *moigonno* "waves"

**Note:** Stems ending in long vowels insert *-h-* before the plural marker:
- *mīw* "fish" → *mīwhen* "fishes"

### Case System

Izaki operates on an eight-case system (*kakuun* 格運):

| Case | Japanese | Function | Marker (V-stem) | Marker (C-stem) |
|------|----------|----------|----------------|----------------|
| **Nominative** | 主格 *shukaku* | Subject/Topic | ∅ | ∅ |
| **Genitive** | 属格 *zukkaku* | Possession | -n | -un |
| **Accusative** | 対格 *taikaku* | Direct object | V̄ (length) | ∅ |
| **Dative** | 与格 *yokaku* | Recipient, direction | -i | -i |
| **Essive** | 所格 *shokaku* | Static location | -s | -us |
| **Allative** | 向格 *hyōnkaku* | Motion toward | -r | -ur |
| **Ablative** | 脱格 *daskaku* | Motion from | -l | -ul |
| **Instrumental** | 具格 *gukaku* | Means, instrument | (vowel progression) | (see below) |

#### Case Declension Examples

**Vowel-stem noun:** *miwa* "water"

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| NOM | miwa | miwaan |
| GEN | miwan | miwain |
| ACC | mīwā | miwaita |
| DAT | miwai | miwahi |
| ESS | miwas | miwais |
| ALL | miwar | miwair |
| ABL | miwal | miwail |
| INST | miwae | miwaei |

**Consonant-stem noun:** *tsokke* "tear"

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| NOM | tsokke | tsokkeen |
| GEN | tsokken | tsokkein |
| ACC | tsokkē | tsokkeita |
| DAT | tsokkei | tsokkehi |
| ESS | tsokkes | tsokkeis |
| ALL | tsokker | tsokkeir |
| ABL | tsokkel | tsokkeil |
| INST | tsokkei | tsokkeii |

#### Instrumental Case Formation

The instrumental employs a vowel-shift pattern:

- **-a** → **-e**: *miwa* → *miwae*
- **-e** → **-i**: *oboe* → *oboei*
- **-i** → **-yo**: *sri* → *sryo*
- **-o** → **-u**: *ahiro* → *ahirou*
- **-u** → **-wa**: *usumu* → *usumwa*

For consonant-final stems, the final consonant geminates before the vowel-shifted ending:
- *shawin* → *shawinno*

#### Derived Cases

Additional semantic cases are formed by suffixing particles to the instrumental stem:

- **Comitative** (accompaniment): INST + *-te*
  - *miwaete* "with water"
- **Translative** (transformation): INST + *-nde*
  - *sryonde* "to become a river"
- **Dedative** (topic): INST + *-nkai*
  - *ahirounkai* "about/regarding color"
- **Abessive** (absence): INST + *-ttan*
  - *saisonuttan* "without love"
- **Instructive** (means): DAT + *-hte*
  - *miwahte* "by means of water"

## Pronominal System

### Personal Pronouns

Izaki distinguishes inclusive/exclusive first person plural:

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|----------|--------|
| 1st | *nai* | *naiin* (exclusive), *nahu* (inclusive) |
| 2nd | *sai* | *saiin* |
| 3rd | *han* | *hannu* |

An honorific second person is formed with the prefix *ki-*: *kisai*, *kisaiin*.

#### Pronominal Case Paradigm

| Case | 1SG | 2SG | 3SG |
|------|-----|-----|-----|
| NOM | nai | sai | han |
| GEN | naiyo | saiyo | haiyo |
| ACC | naka | saka | haka |
| DAT | nakai | sakai | hakai |
| ESS | nais | sais | hais |
| ALL | nair | sair | hair |
| ABL | nail | sail | hail |
| INST | nayo | sayo | hane |

#### Enclitic Possessive Suffixes

Possessive relationships may be encoded via enclitic suffixes rather than genitive pronouns:

| Person | Genitive Form | Enclitic Suffix |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| 1SG | *naiyo* | *-ne* |
| 2SG | *saiyo* | *-se* |
| 3SG | *haiyo* | *-he* |

**Examples:**
- *dyensankine* = *naiyo dyensanki* "my computer"
- *hamikuse* = *saiyo hamiku* "your smile"

#### Accusative Enclitics

Direct object pronouns may attach to transitive verbs:

- *Nai saka saison* = *Nai saisonshi* "I love you"

#### Possessive Construction

Possession is expressed via a dedicated possessive form (*possessor tense*) of pronouns + nominative noun + copula:

| Standard | Possessive Form |
|----------|----------------|
| *nai* | *nara* |
| *sai* | *sara* |
| *han* | *hara* |

**Structure:** [Pronoun.POSS] [Noun.NOM] [Copula]

**Examples:**
- *Nara jityensha yo.* "I have a bicycle."
- *Hara tasuin chuneen yohan.* "She has many clothes."

For non-pronominal possessors, the dative case is employed:
- *Kaotoi takamaan kanki yohan.* "Kaoto has two cars."

### Interrogative Pronouns (*Gimunshi* 疑問詞)

| Interrogative | Meaning |
|---------------|---------|
| *nashi* | what |
| *naka* | who |
| *estin* | when |
| *soi* | how |
| *kami* | where |
| *naki* | why |
| *nalki* | which |

Interrogatives decline for case (except *soi* "how"):

| Case | "what" | "who" | "where" |
|------|--------|-------|---------|
| NOM | nashi | naka | kami |
| GEN | nazhinnan | nagan | kamin |
| ACC | nashii | nakaa | kamii |
| DAT | nai | nakai | — |
| ESS | nazhisnas | nagas | kamikas |

### Indefinite Pronouns

Izaki forms indefinite series through suffixation:

| Base | Negative | Universal | Assertive | Elective |
|------|----------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| *nashi* | *nashehen* (nothing) | *ikonashi* (everything) | *nashiki* (something) | *nashitsas* (anything) |
| *naka* | *nagahen* (nobody) | *ikonaga* (everyone) | *nagaki* (someone) | *nagatsas* (anybody) |
| *kami* | *kamihen* (nowhere) | *kamiden* (everywhere) | *kamiki* (somewhere) | *kamitsas* (anywhere) |

## Verbal Morphology

All Izaki verbs in citation form terminate in *-su*, which represents the infinitive marker. The element preceding *-su* constitutes the verb stem, to which inflectional suffixes attach.

### Verb Classes

Verbs are categorized into three conjugation classes based on stem-final phonology:

1. **Class I (CV-su):** Stems ending in short V + consonant
   - *kadasu* "to arrive", *nugisu* "to clean", *nakosu* "to call"

2. **Class II (VV-su):** Stems ending in long vowel or diphthong
   - *moasu* "to buy", *eisu* "to drink", *jingoasu* "to hurt"

3. **Class III (C-su):** Stems ending in consonant + *-su*
   - *odensu* "to know", *mahsu* "to become", *yosu* "to be"

### Consonant Gradation in Verbs (*Seionkwa* 清音化)

Class I verbs undergo a process termed "purification" (*seionkwa*), whereby stem-final voiced obstruents devoice in certain conjugations:

**Strong stem** (used in perfect, future, participle):
- *zhakusu* → stem *zhaku-*

**Weak stem** (used in present, past, hypothetical):
- *zhakusu* → stem *zhaka-* (voiced *k* → *g*)

**Examples of gradation:**

| Infinitive | Meaning | Strong Stem | Weak Stem |
|------------|---------|-------------|-----------|
| *kadasu* | to arrive | kada- | kata- |
| *nebusu* | to cook | nebu- | nepu- |
| *nugisu* | to clean | nugi- | nuki- |
| *dagasu* | to cut | daga- | daka- |

### Tense-Aspect-Mood System

Izaki distinguishes five primary TAM categories:

1. **Present** (habitual/progressive)
2. **Simple Past** (perfective)
3. **Perfect** (resultative)
4. **Future** (irrealis)
5. **Hypothetical** (counterfactual)

Additionally, two participles exist:
- **Situative** (simultaneous action)
- **Past Participle**

### Person-Number Marking

Verbal suffixes index the person and number of the subject:

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|----------|--------|
| 1st | *-n* | *-nne* |
| 2nd | *-s* | *-sse* |
| 3rd | *∅* (stem) | *-han* |

### Conjugation of *yosu* "to be/exist"

| **Present** | yon | yos | yo | yonne | yosse | yohan |
| **Present NEG** | yonan | yonas | yona | yonanne | yonasse | yonahan |
| **Past** | yoin | yois | yoi | yoinne | yoisse | yoihan |
| **Past NEG** | yonain | yonais | yonai | yonainne | yonaisse | yonaihan |
| **Perfect** | yolen | yoles | yole | yolenne | yolesse | yolehan |

**Morpheme order:** Stem + NEG (*-na-*) + Aspect + Person

### Negation

Negation is marked by the suffix *-na-* inserted immediately after the verb stem:

- *Nai yonan.* "I am not."
- *Han kadanan.* "He does not arrive."

### Politeness Register

Izaki exhibits a three-tier honorific system:

1. **Informal** (*panlihtayu* 半丁寧語): Used with family and close friends
2. **Medium-formal** (*ibukunnirihta* 丁寧語): Default register for acquaintances and strangers
3. **Honorific** (*tsikunnirihta* 敬語): Employed in formal contexts and to show respect

#### Medium-Formal Register

Insert *-ra-* before person marking:

| Tense | 1SG | 2SG | 3SG |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|
| **Present** | yoran | yoras | yora |
| **Past** | yoiran | yoiras | yoira |

**Example:**
- *Nai hagiuppaya yoran.* "I am a teacher (formal)."

#### Honorific Register

Insert *-mine-* (singular) or *-minte-* (plural) before person marking:

| Tense | 1SG | 2SG | 3SG |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|
| **Present** | yominen | yomineas | yomine |
| **Past** | yoiminen | yoimeas | yoimine |

Honorific speech additionally employs distinct pronouns (*kisai*, *kisaiin*).

### Valency-Changing Derivations

Izaki productively derives intransitive (autocausative) verbs from transitive bases through vowel alternation:

| Transitive | Gloss | Intransitive | Gloss |
|------------|-------|--------------|-------|
| *tanayasu* | to close (tr.) | *tanawasu* | to close (intr.) |
| *hankasu* | to lower (tr.) | *hankausu* | to descend (intr.) |
| *runasu* | to lose (tr.) | *runausu* | to get lost (intr.) |
| *taikkasu* | to melt (tr.) | *taikkausu* | to melt (intr.) |

## Adjectival Morphology

Izaki adjectives terminate in *-na* in their attributive form. They decline for case when functioning as predicates or substantives:

**Examples:**
- *tepana* "good"
- *zushina* "bad"
- *nahana* "beautiful"
- *teshikina* "difficult"

When modifying nouns, adjectives precede the head noun and remain uninflected:
- *tepana kato* "a good day"
- *nahana natae* "a beautiful woman"

## Spatial Expressions

### Locational Postpositions

Spatial relationships are expressed through genitive-marked nouns followed by essive-case locational nouns:

| English | Izaki | Structure |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| in front of | *[N.GEN] adaes* | GEN + *adae.ESS* |
| behind | *[N.GEN] odaes* | GEN + *odae.ESS* |
| on | *[N.GEN] uinos* | GEN + *uino.ESS* |
| under | *[N.GEN] teanos* | GEN + *teano.ESS* |
| inside | *[N.GEN] juittes* | GEN + *juitte.ESS* |
| outside | *[N.GEN] togus* | GEN + *toku.ESS* |
| between | *[N.GEN] agis* | GEN + *aki.ESS* |

**Example:**
- *sawohin teanos* "under the table"
- *futagan juittes* "inside the house"

## Syntax

### Basic Word Order

Izaki adheres rigidly to **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb) constituent order:

**Example:**
- *Nai suto yon.*
  - I man am
  - "I am a man."

- *Anna shosess kantsul chubun teki.*
  - Anna novels two.ACC order.PAST
  - "Anna ordered two novels."

### Interrogative Sentences

Yes-no questions are formed by appending the interrogative particle *-ka* to the verb:

- *Se chiketsn dyensha yoraka?*
  - that direct train be.PRES.Q
  - "Is that a direct train?"

Wh-questions employ interrogative pronouns in situ:

- *Kami toilet yoraka?*
  - where toilet be.PRES.Q
  - "Where is the toilet?"

### Existential Constructions

Existence/location employs the copula *yosu* with essive-marked location:

- *Futagas fuwo yoi.*
  - house.ESS bird exist.PAST
  - "There was a bird in the house."
